**Dark spots on melanin-rich skin usually appear after the skin has been irritated or inflamed, and the most effective care is gentle: protect with sunscreen every day, calm breakouts early, stop picking, and use one brightening treatment consistently.** Marks can take time to soften, but a patient, kind ritual makes a real difference to how even your skin looks.

## What causes dark spots on melanin-rich skin?

Hyperpigmentation simply means areas of skin that look darker than the skin around them. On deeper skin tones, the most common type follows inflammation. A pimple, an insect bite, a shaving bump, a burn or even rubbing can prompt the skin to produce extra melanin in that spot. When the original problem heals, a flat brown or dark mark can stay behind.

Other common triggers include:

- **Sun exposure**, which can deepen existing marks and create new uneven areas.
- **Friction**, for example on knees, elbows, knuckles and inner thighs.
- **Hormonal changes**, which can cause patches on the face for some women.
- **Irritating products**, including harsh scrubs or formulas your skin doesn't tolerate.

Because the causes differ, not every dark patch will respond to the same care. That is why persistent or unusual patches deserve a professional look.

## Why is sunscreen so important for dark spots?

UV light encourages pigment production, so unprotected sun exposure can keep marks dark even while you are treating them. Wearing a broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, every morning is the habit that protects all your other efforts. Choose a formula that feels light on your skin so you will reach for it daily, and reapply when you are outside for long periods.

## How should I care for dark spots day to day?

Keep it simple and consistent.

1. **Cleanse gently** twice a day. Skip anything that leaves your face tight or stinging.
2. **Use one brightening treatment.** Vitamin C serums are popular for helping skin look more even. Fruit acids can help with texture, but start slowly.
3. **Moisturise.** A comfortable barrier is less likely to react with new marks.
4. **Protect** with sunscreen every morning.
5. **Exfoliate gently**, once or twice a week at most.

Always patch test new products on a small area for 24 to 48 hours first, and introduce them one at a time.

## Why should I stop picking and scrubbing?

It is tempting to squeeze a pimple or scrub a dark patch, but both create more inflammation, and more inflammation can mean a darker, longer-lasting mark. Hands off is genuinely one of the most effective "treatments" for melanin-rich skin. If breakouts are frequent, focus on calming them early with a gentle, consistent routine, and speak to a dermatologist if they are painful or widespread.

## How long will it take to see a difference?

Be patient. Marks that sit near the surface may begin to look softer within several weeks, while deeper or older marks can take months. Take photos in the same light every two weeks to track progress honestly. Results vary with your skin, the cause of the mark and how consistently you use sunscreen.

## When should I see a dermatologist?

Please see a dermatologist if a patch is:

- spreading, raised, itchy or painful;
- changing in colour, size or shape;
- not improving after two to three months of gentle care and daily sunscreen;
- appearing suddenly in large areas.

A dermatologist can identify the cause and recommend options that skincare alone can't provide.
