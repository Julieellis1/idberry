**Lagos skin needs two moods: light and fresh for the humid, rainy months, and rich and protective for harmattan, which usually runs roughly from November to February.** The one step that never changes is sunscreen. Adjusting your ritual with the seasons keeps skin comfortable, clear-looking and glowing all year, whether you are stuck in traffic on Ikorodu Road or stepping out for an owambe.

## How does Lagos weather affect your skin?

Lagos is warm year-round, but the air changes a lot. In the rainy and humid months, skin deals with sweat, heat and sticky air, which can leave it feeling oily and congested. In harmattan, dry, dusty winds sweep in from the north, humidity drops, and skin can quickly become tight, flaky and ashy. Lips crack, heels dry out, and makeup can sit unevenly.

Your skin is the same skin in both seasons; it just needs different support.

## What should my routine look like in the humid rainy season?

When the air is heavy and you are sweating through the day, lighter is better.

- **Cleanse thoroughly in the evening.** Sweat, sunscreen and city grime build up. A gentle soap or cleanser removes them without stripping. Cleanse again after the gym or a long, hot commute.
- **Choose lighter textures.** Serums and lotions often feel more comfortable than heavy creams. You still need moisture; oily-feeling skin can be dehydrated underneath.
- **Use sunscreen every morning.** Pick a light, non-greasy formula so it sits well in the heat.
- **Keep breakouts calm.** Change pillowcases often, keep phones clean, and avoid touching your face. If you are prone to breakouts, a simple clarifying routine can help.
- **Blot, don't pile on powder.** Blotting papers remove shine without layering product on sweaty skin.

## What should my routine look like during harmattan?

When the dry winds arrive, shift the focus to locking moisture in.

- **Switch to a gentler cleanser** if your usual one leaves skin tight. Avoid very hot water, which strips natural oils.
- **Moisturise on damp skin.** Apply lotion or cream within a few minutes of washing to hold in water.
- **Seal with oil.** A few drops of facial or body oil over your moisturiser, especially at night, helps reduce that ashy look.
- **Go easy on exfoliation.** Dry skin is more easily irritated. Once a week is plenty, and skip it if skin is cracked or sore.
- **Protect the extras.** Lip balm, hand cream, and richer care for elbows, knees and heels.
- **Keep wearing sunscreen.** The haze can make the sun feel softer, but UV still reaches your skin.

Drinking enough water and running a humidifier indoors, if you have one, can also help your skin feel more comfortable.

## Do I still need sunscreen on cloudy or dusty days?

Yes. UV rays pass through clouds and harmattan haze. Sun exposure is a major driver of uneven tone and dark marks on melanin-rich skin, so daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, protects your glow in every season. Reapply if you are outdoors for long periods.

## How do I switch between seasonal routines?

Make changes gradually rather than overnight. As harmattan approaches, add a richer night cream or an oil first, then adjust your cleanser if needed. As humidity returns, lighten up step by step.

Whenever you add a new product, patch test it on a small area for 24 to 48 hours, and introduce one product at a time so you can see how your skin responds. If dryness turns into persistent itching, cracking, rashes or eczema-like patches, or breakouts don't settle in the humid months, please see a dermatologist.
